Volume and Order Flow

Volume counts what was traded. Order flow shows who had to have it, and where the other side stopped giving way.

Sixteen lessons that start with the number under every chart — what volume measures, what it cannot tell you about direction, and how participation is normalized against a baseline before the word "spike" means anything. From there it builds the volume-derived indicators, session and anchored VWAP, and the profile view that reorganizes activity by price instead of time. The last third drops below the bar entirely: the quote and the order types that reach it, the transaction stream, the footprint of executed volume at bid and ask, cumulative delta, and the displayed depth that is intent rather than trade — ending in a rule set precise enough to export. Every lesson is one self-contained interactive page — no accounts, no tracking, nothing to install.

The course

What this course is

One question, taken apart in the order you have to understand it: how much traded, at what price it traded, and which side had to cross the spread to make it happen.

16 lessons · 6 stages

What it teaches

What volume measures and what it does not, the four price-and-participation combinations, relative volume against a baseline, and whether volume confirms a move or contradicts it (01–04). Then the indicators built out of volume: On-Balance Volume, and the close-location family, Accumulation/Distribution and Chaikin Money Flow (05–06). Then value: session VWAP and anchored VWAP (07–08), the volume profile and the value area with its POC, high-volume and low-volume nodes (09–10). Then the market's own plumbing: bid, ask, spread and order types, time and sales, footprint charts and bid-ask delta, cumulative volume delta, and the order book (11–15). Lesson 16 turns all of it into explicit, testable rules.

Who it is for

Anyone who has said "that breakout had volume" without being able to say volume relative to what. It assumes you can read a chart and know what a moving average does; it assumes nothing about order books, tape reading or delta. It also suits the reader who has a footprint chart open and is quietly unsure whether the green number means buyers were stronger — the course is explicit that every trade has both a buyer and a seller, and that aggressor classification describes which side crossed the spread, not an unequal number of participants.

How to work through it

In order, 01 to 16, one stage at a time. Each stage is the prerequisite for the next: the baseline comparison in 03 is what makes the confirmation test in 04 mean anything, VWAP in 07 is the reference anchored VWAP moves in 08, the profile in 09 is what the value area in 10 is drawn on, and the rules in 16 ask for market context, participation, location, aggressive flow and price confirmation — one from almost every stage before it. One lesson per sitting is plenty. Stage 5 is where most readers find out that the tape and the book answer different questions.

Where the definitions come from

The labs are simplified on purpose, so each lesson closes with an official reading link to a primary source rather than to a vendor selling order-flow software: CME Group's education material for how exchange volume is counted, what market-by-order data is, and the difference between market makers and market takers; Nasdaq's own glossary for VWAP; and the SEC's investor.gov for order types. Those are definitions from the venue and the regulator — the course's own synthetic examples are illustrations of them, never a substitute.

Every lesson still works with no network at all. Each page is entirely self-contained, and the reference links are the only things on it that point off the site.

The sixteen lessons, in order

Six stages, grouped the way the sequence itself groups: the volume basics, the indicators derived from volume, VWAP, the profile and its value area, the order-flow tools — book, tape, footprint, delta — and the rule set that turns them into a decision. Educational use only — nothing here is trading advice. This course teaches interpretation of volume, value, executed flow, and displayed depth. It does not provide live market data, trading signals, execution access, profitability claims, or personalized investment advice. Position risk, portfolio risk, drawdown, and risk-of-ruin are reserved for course 6; strategy validation for course 7.

Data and classification scope. All charts, order books, tape data, profiles, and executions in these lessons are deterministic synthetic examples. The course uses stated conventions — relative volume is current volume divided by a comparable baseline volume; OBV adds the full bar volume on an up close and subtracts it on a down close; Accumulation/Distribution and Chaikin Money Flow infer pressure from close location within the bar and are not trade-by-trade aggressor measures; session and anchored VWAP use typical price (high + low + close) / 3 weighted by volume; the profile assigns each bar to price bins using typical price; the value area contains approximately 70% of profile volume around the POC; bid/ask delta is ask-classified volume minus bid-classified volume, and CVD is its running total. Time-and-sales and footprint classifications are simplified and explicitly labeled, and order-book examples show displayed liquidity, which can be added, consumed, moved, or canceled. Real outputs vary by venue, feed, vendor, asset class, session, aggregation, and classification method.

Stage 1 of 6 Lessons 01–04

Volume basics

What the number under the chart counts, and what it refuses to say. Volume measures participation, not direction: a completed trade always has both a buyer and a seller, so a tall bar tells you how much changed hands, never who won. This stage sets the reading habits the rest of the course depends on — pairing price progress with changing activity, normalizing current volume against a comparable baseline before anything gets called a spike, and separating expansion from climax and absorption.

Stage 2 of 6 Lessons 05–06

Volume-derived indicators

Two ways to turn the volume series into a line you can compare with price. On-Balance Volume accumulates the full bar volume by closing direction; Accumulation/Distribution and Chaikin Money Flow weight where price closed inside each bar's range. Both infer pressure from the finished bar, which is exactly why they come before the tape: they are not trade-by-trade aggressor measures, and the lessons say so where it would be easy to imply otherwise.

Stage 3 of 6 Lessons 07–08

VWAP

The average price weighted by volume — what the session actually paid, rather than what it printed last. Lesson 08 moves the starting point to an event you choose, so the question becomes whether price is accepted above or below the average traded value since that event rather than since the open.

Stage 4 of 6 Lessons 09–10

Profile and value area

The same trades, reorganized by price instead of by time. A volume profile bins traded volume into price levels and shows where the market spent its activity; the value area, the point of control, and the high- and low-volume nodes name the locations the auction kept returning to and the ones it passed straight through. In these labs the value area holds approximately 70% of profile volume around the POC — a convention, and the lesson states it as one.

Interop

Lesson 16 exports a rule set

The last lesson is not a quiz you pass and close. It turns what you have learned into explicit conditions, checks them against each other, and then hands the whole rule set back to you as a file, so it can outlive the browser tab it was written in.

Format: volume-order-flow-rule-v1

What comes out of it

Lesson 16 takes the market context you selected, the participation test, the location the setup depends on — above or below VWAP, above or below the value area, or at a structural level — the order-flow condition, the price confirmation, the entry side and trigger, the invalidation, the exit with its minimum reward-to-risk, and the situations you decided not to trade at all, and exports the whole specification as a JSON document stamped volume-order-flow-rule-v1.

The schema linked beside this is the contract for that shape. It is worth reading if you would rather keep your rules in your own notebook or script: match the shape and the same fields carry over. The schema is strict on purpose — additionalProperties: false everywhere — so a field that is not in the contract is a rule that will not validate rather than a silent extra. Note what the export deliberately does NOT contain: any measure of what the rule earned. There is no performance object anywhere in the shape, and minimumRewardRisk is a constraint you set, not a result — so a rule that is merely well specified is never mistaken for one that has been shown to work. Validation is course 7's subject. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded, and there is no account anywhere in this course.
