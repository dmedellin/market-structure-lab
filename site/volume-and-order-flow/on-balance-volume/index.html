On-Balance Volume

Use On-Balance Volume to accumulate volume by closing direction and compare its trend with price.

Review concepts
Market behavior · Volume direction proxy ↕

OBV tracks whether volume is accumulating on up closes or down closes.

Rising OBV means more volume is being added on up-closing bars. Falling OBV means more volume is being subtracted on down-closing bars. It does not identify every trade’s aggressor.

Seller progressBuyer progress
Core concepts

What to understand

Keep the interpretation direct: participation, aggressive side, and actual price progress.

1

Calculation

Add volume when the close rises, subtract it when the close falls, and keep it unchanged on equal closes.

2

Confirmation

Price and OBV making compatible highs or lows supports the observed trend.

3

Divergence

Price progresses while OBV fails to confirm, indicating weaker volume participation.

Reading process

Read it in order

Do not jump from one number to a trade. Describe the evidence first.

1

Read price structure

Mark higher highs, lower lows, or a range.

2

Read OBV structure

Compare OBV swings over the same bars.

3

Describe agreement

Knowledge check

Check your interpretation

Select one answer for each question. Feedback appears immediately.

1. A bar closes above the prior close. OBV does what?

OBV adds the entire bar volume on an up close.

2. Price makes a new high but OBV does not. This is:

Volume participation is not confirming price’s new high, but price confirmation is still required.

3. What matters most about OBV?

OBV is cumulative, so structure and direction are more useful than the raw level.

Practical rules

  • Focus on OBV direction and swings, not its absolute number.
  • Compare matching price and OBV points.
  • Treat divergence as a warning, not an entry by itself.

Common mistakes

  • Calling OBV true buy/sell volume.
  • Comparing OBV values across symbols.
  • Trading every small divergence.
