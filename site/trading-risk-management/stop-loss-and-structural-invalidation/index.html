Stop-Loss and Structural Invalidation

Place the stop where the trade thesis becomes invalid, then size the position to make that structural distance affordable.

Review concepts
Market behavior · Thesis invalidation⊥

In a bullish setup, buyers remain in control while the protected low holds.

A break below the protected low shows that buyers failed to defend the structure. The stop belongs beyond invalidation, not at an arbitrary dollar loss.

Uncontrolled outcomeControlled exposure
Core concepts

What to understand

Keep the process direct: define the risk, calculate it, and enforce the limit.

1

Structural stop

Placed beyond the level whose break invalidates the setup.

2

Noise risk

A stop inside normal price movement may exit while the thesis remains valid.

3

Size adjustment

A wider valid stop requires a smaller position, not a forced tighter stop.

RISK MODEL
Risk process

Apply it in order

Risk calculations should happen before the order is submitted.

1

Identify the protected level

Use the swing or range boundary that must hold for the thesis.

2

Allow for normal movement

Place the exit beyond invalidation rather than directly inside common wick activity.

3

Size from the distance

Calculate quantity only after entry and stop are known.

Knowledge check

Check your understanding

Select one answer for each question. Feedback appears immediately.

1. Where should a structural stop be based?

The chart determines invalidation; account risk determines quantity.

2. A valid stop is wider than expected. What should usually change?

Reduce size so the wider structural stop still fits the risk budget.

3. Price wicks below a minor low but holds the protected low. What happened?

A minor fluctuation can occur without breaking the structural invalidation.

Practical rules

  • Define invalidation before calculating quantity.
  • Use candle-close or wick rules consistently.
  • Include possible slippage beyond the trigger.

Common mistakes

  • Placing stops where the loss feels comfortable.
  • Moving a stop farther after entry.
  • Using the same percentage-distance stop for every chart.
