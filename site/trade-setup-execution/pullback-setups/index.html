Pullback Setups

Evaluate corrections within an established trend using structural validity, planned location, pullback quality, and a defined recovery trigger.

Core concepts

Use these concepts as explicit rules rather than changing them after seeing the outcome.

T

Trend first

A pullback setup requires an established impulse and a protected swing.

L

Location

The correction should reach a preplanned area rather than being bought or sold at an arbitrary price.

R

Recovery

Entry depends on the selected model: touch, reaction, local shift, or continuation.

Pullback setup process

Treat depth as context and use structural validity as the decision boundary.

1

Confirm the impulse

Identify the meaningful trend leg and protected structural swing.

2

Define the pullback area

Mark prior breakout structure, support or resistance, or another planned zone.

3

Monitor validity

Reject the original setup if the protected swing is broken.

4

Require recovery evidence

Use the exact reaction or structure-shift rule in the entry model.

Pullback components

✓
Established impulseThe trend produced a meaningful higher high or lower low before correcting.
✓
Planned locationThe correction reaches prior structure, breakout area, or another defined zone.
!
Controlled correctionDepth and momentum should not already invalidate the protected swing.
✓
Recovery triggerThe selected entry model receives its required reaction or local structure shift.

Common mistakes

1
Buying or selling because price moved farDistance traveled does not prove the pullback has ended.
2
Calling structural failure a deeper pullbackOnce the protected swing breaks, the original thesis must be reassessed.
3
Entering at a location without a triggerA zone can fail; the entry model determines how much confirmation is required.
4
Using one retracement percentage in every marketVolatility, timeframe, and structure change what “deep” means.
