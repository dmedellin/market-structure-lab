Position sizing process

The position quantity is an output of risk rules, not an input chosen from conviction.

1

Set account risk

Use equity and a predefined risk percentage or fixed dollar amount.

2

Calculate per-unit loss

Measure share risk or planned premium loss and include expected costs.

3

Divide and round down

Never round up beyond the account risk budget.

4

Check concentration and liquidity

A risk-sized trade can still be too concentrated or difficult to execute.

Sizing formulas

ACCOUNT RISK = EQUITY × RISK % SHARES = floor( ACCOUNT RISK ÷ (|ENTRY − STOP| + COST PER SHARE) ) LONG OPTION CONTRACTS = floor( ACCOUNT RISK ÷ PLANNED LOSS PER CONTRACT )
Options limitation: An underlying-price stop does not map to one guaranteed option price. Delta, gamma, implied volatility, time decay, spread, and gaps can all change the realized premium exit.

Common mistakes

1
Choosing quantity before the stopThis forces structure to fit a preferred position rather than controlling risk.
2
Ignoring gaps and slippageActual losses can exceed planned stop distance, especially around events or in illiquid instruments.
3
Treating option premium and underlying movement as one-to-oneOption pricing introduces additional sources of risk and execution variance.
4
Using maximum buying power as position sizeAvailable leverage is not the same as acceptable loss.
