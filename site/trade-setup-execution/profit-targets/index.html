Profit Targets

Select profit objectives from opposing structure, measure them in R, and define whether the plan uses a single target, scaling, or a trailing remainder.

Core concepts

Use these concepts as explicit rules rather than changing them after seeing the outcome.

S

Structural objectives

Use prior highs, lows, range edges, and other opposing levels as target references.

R

R-multiple measurement

Measure reward relative to initial risk without allowing the desired ratio to invent a target.

M

Management method

Decide in advance whether to exit once, scale out, or trail a remainder.

Profit target process

Plan exits from market structure first and use R multiples to evaluate the resulting opportunity.

1

Identify opposing levels

Mark the nearest and extended structural objectives.

2

Measure reward in R

Compare each objective with entry-to-stop risk.

3

Choose an exit method

Use one target, scale-out percentages, or a tested trailing rule.

4

Define what changes the plan

Specify whether new structure permits trailing or whether the original objective remains fixed.

Target methods

MethodBest useMain cost
Single structural targetClear opposing level or range objectiveNo participation beyond the target
Scale outTwo meaningful objectives or uncertain continuationReduces full-position reward when price trends strongly
Trail remainderTrend continuation with expanding structureGives back open profit before exit

Common mistakes

1
Inventing a target to reach a preferred R multipleVisible opposing structure may make the planned reward unrealistic.
2
Moving the target farther after entryThis can turn a successful plan into an untested attempt to capture more.
3
Taking profit from fear without a ruleUnplanned exits make expectancy difficult to measure.
4
Assuming scaling always improves resultsScaling reduces risk and volatility but can also reduce average win size.
