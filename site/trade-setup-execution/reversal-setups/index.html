Reversal Setups

Distinguish a temporary countertrend reaction from a reversal by requiring a break of prior structure, a retest, and opposite-side continuation.

Review the process
Synthetic educational examples

Core concepts

Use these concepts as explicit rules rather than changing them after seeing the outcome.

P

Prior trend

A reversal setup begins with a mature trend approaching a meaningful location.

S

Structure change

The countertrend move must break a structural level that mattered to the prior trend.

N

New structure

A retest and opposite-side swing provide evidence and a usable invalidation point.

Reversal setup process

Wait for evidence of control changing instead of predicting the exact top or bottom.

1

Identify the prior trend

Mark the protected swing and the structural level that would signal loss of control.

2

Observe the reaction

Look for rejection, sweep, exhaustion, or failure at a meaningful area.

3

Require a structure break

A countertrend candle is not enough; the prior sequence must be disrupted.

4

Use the retest and continuation

Confirm an opposite-side swing, define invalidation, and verify room to target.

Confirmation hierarchy

WeakOne countertrend candle
DevelopingSweep or exhaustion at a key level
StrongerBreak of countertrend structure
Confirmed modelRetest holds and continuation begins
Reversal is a process. The exact labels vary, but the evidence should show that the prior side lost control and the opposite side established structure.

Common mistakes

1
Calling every large countertrend candle a reversalSize alone does not show that prior structure has changed.
2
Entering before the structural breakPredicting the turning point increases false starts and leaves invalidation unclear.
3
Ignoring a failed retestIf price cannot hold the reclaimed level, the reversal model may be invalid.
4
Using reversal and pullback labels interchangeablyThe two setups require different context, triggers, and failure conditions.
