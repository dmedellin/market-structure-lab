Trade Management

Manage an open position using predefined milestones for holding, reducing, moving the stop, trailing, or exiting, and score discipline separately from outcome.

Core concepts

Use these concepts as explicit rules rather than changing them after seeing the outcome.

P

Predefined plan

Choose management milestones before entry instead of reacting to each candle emotionally.

S

Stop only tightens

A stop may remain or move toward reduced risk; it should not move farther after entry.

O

Outcome is separate

A poor decision can make money and a correct decision can lose. Review both process and result.

Trade management process

Use observable price or R milestones and preserve the original invalidation rule.

1

Start with the original plan

Record stop, targets, scale percentages, and trailing conditions before entry.

2

Act only at milestones

Use predefined structure or R events to change the position.

3

Never expand planned risk

Do not widen the stop or add risk after invalidation approaches.

4

Review execution separately

Score whether the plan was followed before judging the financial result.

Management priorities

1
Preserve invalidationNever move a stop farther away to keep a losing thesis alive.
2
Follow predefined milestonesUse structure or R thresholds that were chosen before entry.
3
Record deviationsSeparate planned management from discretionary changes in the journal.
4
Evaluate across many tradesA single outcome cannot prove whether a management rule is superior.

Common mistakes

1
Moving to break-even too earlyNormal volatility can remove a valid trade before the planned trigger occurs.
2
Widening the stop to avoid a lossThis converts controlled risk into an unplanned larger loss.
3
Taking partial profit without a repeatable ruleRandom scaling changes average wins and makes backtesting unreliable.
4
Optimizing management from one memorable tradeRules require a broad sample across different market paths.
