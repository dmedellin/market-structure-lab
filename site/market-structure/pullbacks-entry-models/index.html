Pullbacks & entry models

A correct directional bias can still produce a poor trade when the entry is late, premature, or structurally invalid. Compare earlier entries with confirmation-based and continuation entries.

See the entry sequence
Earlier price ↔ less confirmation

Three entry families

None is universally best. Each exchanges entry price, confirmation, and distance to invalidation.

A

Aggressive

Enter at the expected reaction zone before a lower-timeframe shift. Better location, but more false starts.

C

Confirmation

Wait for a reaction, local structure shift, and a defined swing that can serve as invalidation.

B

Continuation breakout

Enter as price resumes through a continuation level. Stronger proof, but usually a later price and wider chase risk.

The entry sequence

Direction alone is not an entry. Require a useful location, a reaction, a trigger, and an explicit invalidation.

1

Trend

Identify the active higher-timeframe structure and the protected swing that keeps the thesis alive.

2

Location

Wait for price to reach a prior breakout, range edge, support/resistance zone, or another preplanned area.

3

Reaction + trigger

Choose whether your model enters on touch, on a local shift, or only after continuation resumes.

4

Invalidation

Define the price that proves the entry model wrong, then size the trade around that distance.

Common mistakes

1
Buying because price “looks cheap”A deep pullback is not automatically attractive; it may already have invalidated the trend.
2
Waiting for so much proof that location disappearsMore confirmation can reduce uncertainty while also worsening entry price and reward-to-risk.
3
Moving the model after entryChoose touch, confirmation, or continuation before the setup occurs and evaluate that model consistently.
4
Ignoring the failed pullbackOnce the protected structural level breaks, “waiting for a better entry” can become denial of invalidation.
