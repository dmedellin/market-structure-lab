Options Trading

A contract has terms, a price, and a clock. Learn to read all three before you ever quote one.

Sixteen lessons that start with what an option contract actually obliges each side to do, then take apart the premium you pay for it — intrinsic and extrinsic value, the option chain and its spread, time decay, implied volatility, and the four Greeks that measure them. From there it builds the five positions this course teaches end to end, the exercise and assignment mechanics that decide what you end up holding, and a planning lesson that scores a complete plan and exports it. Every lesson is one self-contained interactive page — no accounts, no tracking, nothing to install.

The course

What this course is

One instrument, taken apart in the order you have to understand it: what you are buying, what it costs and why, what you can build with it, and what happens when it expires.

16 lessons · 4 stages

What it teaches

The contract itself: its terms, the four call and put positions, moneyness, the premium, and the chain it is quoted on (01–05). Then what moves that premium: expiration and time decay, implied volatility, delta and gamma, theta and vega (06–09). Then five positions built from those parts — long calls and puts, covered calls, cash-secured puts, and both vertical spreads (10–14). Then what settles it: exercise, assignment and expiration handling, and a scored trade plan (15–16).

Who it is for

Anyone who can read a chart and now wants to understand the instrument that trades on top of it — including the reader who has bought a call, watched the stock go the right way, and still lost money. It starts from contract terms, so no prior options knowledge is assumed. It also suits someone who already trades options and cannot say, without guessing, which part of a premium is intrinsic and which part is the clock.

How to work through it

In order, 01 to 16, one stage at a time. Each stage is the prerequisite for the next: the Greeks in 08–09 only mean something once 04 has split the premium in two, the strategies in 10–14 assume you can already price the legs, and the plan in 16 asks for a spread cost, a risk budget and an exit rule the earlier lessons taught you to compute. One lesson per sitting is plenty. Stage 4 is what stops the first fourteen from being trivia.

Curriculum

The sixteen lessons, in order

Four stages, grouped the way the sequence itself groups: what the contract is, what prices it, what you can build with it, and how it ends. Each lesson is a single self-contained page of interactive charts, worked explanations, and practice. Start at 01 and work down.

Educational use only — nothing here is trading advice. Options involve risk and are not suitable for every investor. This course is educational: it provides no personalized investment advice and no live trading signals.

Modeling scope. Every price, chain, quote, scenario and outcome in these lessons is synthetic, and theoretical values come from a simplified European Black–Scholes model. The course does not model every listed-product feature, dividend, early-exercise decision, fee, tax, margin rule or market microstructure effect. Real results can differ because of spread, slippage, commissions, gaps, liquidity, assignment, exercise, implied volatility and time decay. Verify contract specifications, brokerage procedures, costs and the current Options Disclosure Document independently before trading.

Stage 2 of 4 Lessons 06–09

Pricing and the Greeks

Why the premium moves when the stock does not. The clock first — how extrinsic value drains as expiration approaches — then implied volatility on its own terms, then the sensitivities that put numbers on both: delta and gamma for direction, theta and vega for time and volatility. Each lesson checks its estimate against a full repricing, so you can see where a linear approximation stops being true.

Interop

Lesson 16 exports a trade plan

The last lesson is not a quiz you pass and close. It scores a complete plan and then hands it back to you as a file, so the plan can outlive the browser tab it was written in.

Format: options-trade-plan-v1

What comes out of it

Lesson 16 takes the thesis, the contract, the risk budget and the exit rules you enter, scores them across seven checks, and exports the whole plan as a JSON document stamped options-trade-plan-v1. The file records the inputs, the computed metrics — breakeven, maximum loss, spread cost, risk budget — and the score with the individual checks that produced it, so a plan you approved can be read back later against what actually happened.

The schema linked beside this is the contract for that shape. It is worth reading if you would rather keep your plans in your own spreadsheet or script: match the shape and the same fields carry over. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded, and there is no account anywhere in this course.
