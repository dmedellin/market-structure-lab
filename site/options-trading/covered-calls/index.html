Covered Calls

Combine owned shares with a short call, calculate premium-adjusted breakeven and capped maximum profit, and evaluate assignment and downside exposure.

Review the process
Synthetic educational examples

Core concepts

Learn the mechanics, calculate the outcome, and connect the contract to a defined risk limit.

1

Position structure

A covered call combines long shares with a short call representing the same number of shares.

2

Income and basis

Premium received lowers the expiration breakeven by the premium amount.

3

Capped upside

Above the strike, further stock gains are offset by the short call, while downside in the shares remains substantial.

Plan a covered call

The strategy is appropriate only when selling the shares at the strike is acceptable.

1

Confirm share coverage

Own the correct number of deliverable shares for the calls sold.

2

Choose an acceptable strike

The strike is a potential sale price, not merely a premium-selection input.

3

Calculate adjusted basis

Subtract premium from stock basis to estimate expiration breakeven.

4

Plan assignment and exit

Know how dividends, early assignment, and expiration can affect the stock position.

Common mistakes

1
Selling calls on shares that must not be soldAssignment can remove the shares at the strike.
2
Calling the strategy downside protectionPremium only offsets a limited amount of stock loss.
3
Ignoring tax and account consequencesAssignment and share disposition can have account-specific effects that require professional review.
