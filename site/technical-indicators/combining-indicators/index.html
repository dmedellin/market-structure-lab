Combining Indicators Without Redundancy

Group indicators by function, compare normalized outputs and correlations, build a compact set for a defined purpose, and avoid stacking multiple transformations of the same price behavior as false confirmation.

Core concepts

Understand what the indicator calculates, how its parameters affect it, and what it cannot establish by itself.

Functional families

Trend, momentum, volatility, strength, and breakout indicators answer different questions.

2

Redundancy

Indicators built from similar inputs and smoothing can move together. Agreement may be repeated evidence rather than independent confirmation.

Lesson rule

Multiple indicators do not create independent confirmation when they transform the same data in similar ways and serve the same job.

Common mistakes

1
Stacking RSI, stochastic, and ROC as three votesAll three are momentum-related transformations of price and can repeat similar information.
2
Adding indicators without a decision roleA line that never changes entry, exit, filter, or risk logic adds complexity without function.
3
Confusing normalized display with original unitsNormalization helps compare shapes but changes the scale and must not replace the actual calculation.

Completion standard

✓
Finish when every selected indicator has a unique job.

Choose a market purpose, select a compact set, identify each family, inspect high correlations, remove redundant tools, and explain exactly how each remaining output changes a decision.

Calculation note: Correlation in this lesson is calculated on synthetic, normalized valid outputs and is descriptive rather than a universal property. Correlation changes with market path, period, and transformation.
