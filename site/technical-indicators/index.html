The course

What this course is

One family of tools, taken apart in the order you have to understand it: what the calculation does, what each family measures, and how to combine them without fooling yourself.

16 lessons · 5 stages

What it teaches

The transformation itself — lookback, responsiveness, warm-up, context (01). Then trend: simple and exponential moving averages and the crossover filters built on them (02–03). Then what momentum and trend strength actually measure: RSI, the stochastic oscillator, MACD, and the directional-movement family (04–07). Then volatility and the channels drawn from it: true range and ATR, Bollinger Bands, Keltner and Donchian channels (08–11). Then the reading skills that decide whether any of it helps: rate of change, divergence, redundancy, regime selection, and deterministic rules (12–16).

Who it is for

Anyone who has an indicator on their chart and could not, right now, write down its formula or say what makes it lag. It assumes you can read a chart and starts from the calculation, so no prior indicator knowledge is needed. It also suits the reader who has six indicators open and suspects — correctly — that four of them are showing the same thing twice.

How to work through it

In order, 01 to 16, one stage at a time. Each stage is the prerequisite for the next: the smoothing in 02 is the basis every later envelope is built on, the divergence work in 13 needs the oscillators from 04–06, and the rules in 16 ask for the trend, strength and volatility filters that 03, 07 and 08–11 taught you to compute. One lesson per sitting is plenty. Stage 5 is what stops the first eleven from being a list of formulas.

Curriculum

The sixteen lessons, in order

Five stages, grouped the way the sequence itself groups: what an indicator is, the trend tools, the momentum and strength tools, the volatility and channel tools, and the synthesis that turns all of them into a decision. Each lesson is a single self-contained page of interactive charts, worked explanations, and practice. Start at 01 and work down.

Start at lesson 01

Educational use only — nothing here is trading advice. This course teaches indicator calculation, interpretation, and rule specification. It does not provide live data, trading signals, profitability claims, or personalized investment advice. Backtesting, costs, robustness, and automation are covered in later courses.

Calculation scope. Every price series in these lessons is a deterministic synthetic example. The course uses standard conventions — an equal-weight rolling window for the SMA, an EMA seeded with the first complete SMA, Wilder smoothing for RSI, ATR and ADX, a population standard deviation for Bollinger Bands, and a prior completed channel for Donchian breakout signals. Small differences from charting platforms can occur because of source, seed, rounding, missing-bar, adjustment, or incomplete-bar conventions. Indicators transform historical price data; they do not guarantee future direction.

Interop

Lesson 16 exports a rule set

The last lesson is not a quiz you pass and close. It turns what you have learned into explicit conditions, checks them against each other, and then hands the whole rule set back to you as a file, so it can outlive the browser tab it was written in.

Format: technical-indicator-rule-v1

What comes out of it

Lesson 16 takes the direction, the trend, strength and volatility filters, the entry trigger, the exit and the parameters you set, marks the entry and exit events on a synthetic series, and exports the whole specification as a JSON document stamped technical-indicator-rule-v1. The file records the data assumptions it was written against, the rule itself, and diagnostics — the clarity score, whether direction and trend agree, and the events it produced — so a rule you wrote can be read back later and reproduced exactly.

The schema linked beside this is the contract for that shape. It is worth reading if you would rather keep your rules in your own notebook or script: match the shape and the same fields carry over. Note what the export deliberately does NOT contain — any measure of profitability. The diagnostics carry performanceEvaluated: false on purpose, so a rule that is merely well specified is never mistaken for a rule that has been shown to work. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded, and there is no account anywhere in this course.
